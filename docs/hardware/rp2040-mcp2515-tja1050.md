# RP2040 + MCP2515/TJA1050 hardware setup

## Scope

This note describes the first USB-powered CAN prototype for the XA50 diagnostic project. The known CAN module combines:

- an MCP2515 Classical-CAN controller;
- a TJA1050 high-speed CAN transceiver; and
- a jumper-selectable 120-ohm resistor between `CANH` and `CANL`.

The separate MCP2551 board is not used. The prototype will connect to the stationary vehicle through DLC3/OBD-II and to the Windows PC through the RP2040's native USB interface.

## Planned topology

```text
Windows PC
    |
    | USB data and power
    v
RP2040 board (3.3-V GPIO)
    |
    | SPI + interrupt through logic-level translation
    v
MCP2515 + TJA1050 module (5-V supply)
    |
    | CANH, CANL, ground
    v
OBD-II male plug -> vehicle DLC3
```

## Important module settings

- Supply the MCP2515/TJA1050 module with **5 V**, unless inspection of the exact board proves that it has separate controller and transceiver supply rails. The TJA1050 itself requires 5 V.
- Leave the module's **120-ohm termination jumper open/disabled** when connected to the vehicle. The vehicle bus is already terminated at its two ends, and the OBD interface is only a diagnostic stub.
- Record the oscillator marking on the module. It is commonly 8 MHz or 16 MHz, and the firmware CAN timing must use the actual value.
- Do not connect the separate MCP2551 transceiver.

The termination jumper is closed only when constructing a separate bench CAN bus and this module is physically one of that bus's two terminated ends.

## Parts and tools

Required:

- RP2040 development board with native USB;
- combined MCP2515/TJA1050 module;
- suitable bidirectional arrangement of 3.3-V/5-V **logic-level translators**;
- USB data cable;
- OBD-II male plug;
- short twisted pair for `CANH` and `CANL`;
- hookup wire and strain relief;
- multimeter with voltage, resistance, and continuity modes.

Useful but optional:

- logic analyzer or oscilloscope;
- current-limited bench supply for off-car testing;
- USB isolator for a more robust PC/vehicle connection.

A DC power converter or regulator is not a substitute for a logic-level translator. Common BSS138-based I2C level-shifter boards are not preferred for push-pull SPI. Confirm the exact converter part before connecting it.

## Power and grounding

The RP2040 uses 3.3-V GPIO. The combined CAN module is expected to run its MCP2515 and TJA1050 from 5 V.

For the first prototype:

1. Power the RP2040 through its USB connector.
2. Supply the CAN module from a single regulated USB-derived 5-V source, such as the RP2040 board's documented USB `VBUS`/`5V` output if available and adequately rated.
3. Connect RP2040 ground, translator ground, CAN-module ground, and OBD pin 5 together.
4. Do not connect another 5-V source at the same time, to avoid back-powering the USB host or RP2040 board.
5. Leave OBD pin 16 disconnected. Do not expose this breadboard prototype to vehicle battery power or automotive transients.

The exact RP2040 power pin must be checked against the development board's schematic before wiring.

## Logic-level translation

If the MCP2515 is powered at 5 V, all exposed SPI and interrupt signals must be treated as 5-V logic. Translate them as follows:

| Direction | RP2040 side | CAN-module side | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| RP2040 -> module | SPI clock | `SCK` | 3.3 V to 5 V |
| RP2040 -> module | SPI MOSI | `SI`/`MOSI` | 3.3 V to 5 V |
| RP2040 -> module | chip select GPIO | `CS` | 3.3 V to 5 V |
| Module -> RP2040 | SPI MISO | `SO`/`MISO` | 5 V to 3.3 V |
| Module -> RP2040 | interrupt GPIO | `INT` | 5 V to 3.3 V |

If the selected translator has `LV`, `HV`, `OE`, and ground pins:

- connect `LV` to the RP2040's regulated 3.3 V;
- connect `HV` to the module's regulated 5 V;
- connect both sides to the same ground;
- place `OE` in the manufacturer's defined enabled state; and
- verify that each channel supports the required fixed direction and push-pull SPI operation.

Start with a conservative SPI clock, approximately 500 kHz to 1 MHz. Increase it only after loopback and register-read tests are reliable.

Do not rely on an untranslated 3.3-V signal driving a 5-V MCP2515. At 5-V supply, its `SCK`, `SI`, and `CS` guaranteed input-high threshold is 0.7 times its supply, or 3.5 V. Its `SO` and `INT` outputs can also rise near 5 V and must not directly drive RP2040 GPIO.

## OBD-II plug wiring

For the USB-powered prototype, populate only these three vehicle connections:

| OBD-II pin | Vehicle signal | CAN-module connection |
|---:|---|---|
| 5 | Signal ground | Module/interface ground |
| 6 | CAN-H | `CANH` |
| 14 | CAN-L | `CANL` |

Leave these unconnected initially:

| OBD-II pin | Reason |
|---:|---|
| 4 | Chassis ground is unnecessary for the first signal connection. |
| 16 | Unprotected battery positive must not power the breadboard prototype. |

OBD connector drawings are easily mirrored between the mating face and solder side. Use the molded contact numbers on the actual plug. Before connecting it to the car, use continuity mode to prove which solder terminal reaches pins 5, 6, and 14.

Use a short twisted pair for pins 6 and 14. Provide insulation and strain relief so movement cannot short adjacent contacts.

## Off-car inspection checklist

Do not attach the prototype to the car until these checks pass:

- [ ] Photograph both sides of the CAN module and record all IC markings.
- [ ] Record the oscillator marking and resulting frequency.
- [ ] Identify whether the CAN module has one common 5-V supply or separate rails.
- [ ] Record the exact logic-level translator part/module.
- [ ] Verify the translator supports push-pull SPI in both required directions.
- [ ] Confirm the termination jumper is **open**.
- [ ] With the loose module unpowered, confirm `CANH` to `CANL` is high resistance with the jumper open and approximately 120 ohms with it closed.
- [ ] Verify the OBD plug's pins 5, 6, and 14 by continuity from the mating contacts to the solder terminals.
- [ ] Confirm there is no connection to OBD pin 16.
- [ ] Check for shorts between 5 V, 3.3 V, and ground before applying power.

## Bench bring-up sequence

1. Leave the CAN module disconnected from the vehicle.
2. Power the RP2040, translator, and module from USB-derived rails.
3. Measure approximately 3.3 V on the translator low-voltage rail and 5 V on its high-voltage/module rail.
4. Reset the MCP2515 and read back known registers over SPI.
5. Verify the configured oscillator value matches the physical module.
6. Put the MCP2515 into internal loopback mode and exchange test frames. No CAN wiring or termination is required for internal loopback.
7. Exercise the USB link between the RP2040 and PC using loopback frames.
8. Remove power and perform a final OBD continuity and short-circuit check.

Only after the bench sequence passes should the module be connected to the stationary vehicle in MCP2515 listen-only mode at 500 kbit/s. Active diagnostic transmission comes later and remains restricted to the project's explicit request allowlist.

## Information still needed for the exact pin map

- exact RP2040 development-board model;
- clear photographs of both sides of the MCP2515/TJA1050 module;
- oscillator marking;
- exact 5-V/3.3-V logic-level translator model; and
- clear photographs or pin labels for the OBD-II male plug.

Once recorded, these details will determine the physical RP2040 GPIO assignment, translator channel assignment, power pin, SPI controller, and firmware configuration.
